# Spaces: what each one is, what its axes are, how it moves, how to look at it

Added 2026-11-19 (v1.2.0) at Cody's request: "other spaces ... they all have different movements, different axes ... the axes are not 'lines', they are hyper-complex fields, not just an axis."
Status labels: ESTABLISHED (textbook), AUTHOR (Cody's identification, pieces standard), RECORD (what the repo says), STUB (named, not built), MEASURED (run on 2026-11-19 with the repo's own code).

## 0. The principle: an axis is (algebra, field, view), not a line

In SKILL.md §1 a viewport has axes x, y, z. In the spaces below a coordinate can be a **complex number, a quaternion, an octonion or sedenion, a function (a field over another space), or a whole pencil of circles**. So every axis is declared by three things:

| part | question | examples |
|---|---|---|
| algebra | what values does a coordinate take? | ℝ; ℂ (Hilbert amplitudes, the c-plane); ℍ; 𝕆; 𝕊 (16 components) |
| field | is the value fixed, or does it vary over another space? | a number; a wavefunction ψ(x); Γ(z) over the impedance half-plane; a section of the Hopf fibration |
| view | which real components do I show, and how do the rest enter? | 1–3 real components as geometry; phase as hue and modulus as lightness (domain colouring, ℂ); the remaining components by slicing, small multiples or animation |

Budget (SKILL.md §2): a viewer reads about three real axes metrically. A ℂ axis costs two, an ℍ axis four, an 𝕆 axis eight, an 𝕊 axis sixteen: so every extra algebra dimension has to be spent as colour, slice, time or small-multiple, and the figure must say which components are shown. Pair this skill with `observer-position` (orientation and aperture when there is no camera).

Every space below is described with the same eight fields: points · axes · motions · invariant · views · acceptance test · repo engine · status.

## 1. Minkowski space (see SKILL.md §6)
Points: events (ct, x, y). Axes: ℝ. Motions: boosts, rotations, translations (Poincaré). Invariant: s² = −(ct)² + x² + y². Views: spacetime diagram (45° light cones), observer's past cone. Test: `assert_minkowski_sound()` in `assets/minkowski.py`. Status: ESTABLISHED; implemented as an asset.
**What it is and is not in the Standard-Model engine (2026-11-19):** `ValaQuenta/modules/lagrangian_sm` does not carry Minkowski space; the metric η = diag(+,−,−,−) appears only in two comparison helpers (`compare.py`). The path-of-action engine's Lorentzian signature is the **homogeneous mode** (spatially uniform field) of the Minkowski action ∫d⁴x[½(∂_tφ)² − V], i.e. motion in field space against time; its Euclidean signature is the Wick rotation t → −iτ (motion in −V). A full field-theory Minkowski picture (φ(x, t)) is not built.

## 2. De Sitter space dS_n
Points: events on the hyperboloid −X₀² + X₁² + … + X_n² = ℓ² in ℝ^{1,n}. Axes: ℝ (the embedding), or (t, r, angles) in a patch. Motions: SO(1,n) boosts and rotations; in the flat slicing the dilation t → t + c, x → e^{−Hc}x (H = 1/ℓ). Invariant: the embedding interval (MEASURED: dS₂ point has I = 1.000000000000 before and after a boost). Views: (a) the hyperboloid in 3D for dS₂, (b) the static patch (t, r) with the cosmological horizon at r = ℓ and Gibbons–Hawking temperature 1/(2πℓ) (= 0.159155 for ℓ = 1), (c) the conformal (Penrose) square, (d) flat slicing with e^{2Ht}. Euclidean continuation: the sphere S^n. Test: the interval is invariant; the horizon sits at r = ℓ; the Euclidean section closes into S^n.
Repo: `ValaQuenta/modules/desitter_cavitation` (RECORD: the black-hole interior as a finite sub-Planckian de Sitter core, the Abrikosov vortex core made gravitational). Status: ESTABLISHED; recipe only, no asset yet.

## 3. Hilbert space
Points: **rays** of a complex vector space with an inner product (projective space CP^{n−1}). Axes: each basis vector carries a **complex amplitude**: an axis is a ℂ-line, and in L²(ℝ) each basis vector is itself a function (a field). Motions: unitary U(n) (a rotation that preserves ⟨ψ|φ⟩), projection (measurement), time evolution e^{−iHt/ħ}. Invariant: inner products, so probabilities |⟨φ|ψ⟩|². Metric: Fubini–Study. Views: n = 2 the Bloch sphere; spin-j the Husimi Q-function on the sphere; any n one Argand diagram per amplitude, or the probability simplex with phase as hue; for L² the wavefunction as a domain-coloured curve (modulus = height, phase = colour). Test: normalisation preserved under every motion; global phase invisible.
Repo: `h_rb_hat`, `berry_keating` (the Hilbert–Pólya reading: zeros as eigenvalues of a self-adjoint operator); `circle_atlas` viewports. Status: ESTABLISHED; recipe only.

## 4. Fano plane PG(2,2)
Points: 7 points, 7 lines, 3 points on a line, 3 lines through a point; no metric: purely combinatorial (a finite geometry). Axes: none; the "coordinates" are the seven imaginary units of the octonions. Motions: collineations, the group PSL(2,7) ≅ GL(3,2) (order 168), acting on the lines as permutations; the cyclic shift i → i+1 (mod 7) on lines {i, i+1, i+3}. MEASURED: every pair of points lies on exactly one line; the line set of `lagrangian` FANO_LINES, (0,1,3), (1,2,4), (2,3,5), (3,4,6), (4,5,0), (5,6,1), (6,0,2), has exactly **168** automorphisms (all 5040 permutations of the seven points tried). Views: the triangle with its three medians and inscribed circle (the seven lines), with orientation arrows giving e_i e_j = ± e_k; the 7-gon with the seven triples. Test: 7/7/3/3 incidence, 168 automorphisms.
Repo: `box_kite` (RECORD: the zero-divisor object is PSL(2,7), Aut(Fano) = GL(3,2), not G₂), `lagrangian` (FANO_LINES). Status: ESTABLISHED.

## 5. Sedenion space, with the bracketing and firing-order engines
Points: 16-component vectors in the Cayley–Dickson algebra 𝕊 (e₀ real, e₁…e₁₅). Axes: **sixteen components, each a real number here, but the unit of an axis is itself an algebra element**; the space is not associative, so **the bracketing is part of the state**: (ab)c ≠ a(bc). Motions: left and right multiplication, conjugation, CD doubling; the order in which factors are multiplied. MEASURED with `emerger.cd_mul` (2026-11-19): octonion basis triples that fail to associate 168 of 343, sedenion 1848 of 3375; **zero divisors** (e_i ± e_j)(e_k ± e_l) = 0: **84 distinct** over **42** index pairs (336 ordered zero-product pairs); none in the octonions (a division algebra). Views: the 16-spoke polyline (σ_RB per channel), the seven box-kites (octahedra of six assessors each) with Fano labels, the zero-divisor locus as a graph; one view per bracketing.
Engines: `emerger` (RECORD: brackets a 16-vector five ways, {1:15} {2:14} {8:8} {4:4:4:4} {4:8:4}, classifies each group C/H/O/FRAGMENT by closure, walks the brackets in a σ_RB-phased firing order), `bracketing_firing_order` (unordered groupings, sequenced orderings, set-membership memory), `box_kite`, `tier8_sedenion`. Test: |ab| = |a||b| fails; the counts above; the five bracketings partition the 16 components.
Status: ESTABLISHED (algebra) + RECORD (the engines). The firing order is the author's.

## 6. Mandelbrot–Julia space
Two linked planes, both ℂ: the **parameter plane** (c) and the **dynamical plane** (z). The map f_c(z) = z² + c is not invertible. M = {c : the orbit of 0 stays bounded}; J_c is the boundary of the points whose orbit stays bounded; **J_c is connected exactly when c ∈ M**. Axes: two complex lines (so four real dimensions, shown as two planes side by side). Motions: iterate f_c; travel in c (watch J_c change); zoom (self-similar: the same structure repeats at smaller scale). Views: M with the current c marked next to J_c; escape-time colouring; external rays; the orbit as a cobweb or a polyline. MEASURED: c = 0.25 bounded and c = 0.2501 escapes (the cusp); c = −2 bounded and c = −2.01 escapes (M ∩ ℝ = [−2, ¼]); c = −1.1 (inside the period-2 disc |c + 1| < ¼) bounded; the main cardioid c(θ) = e^{iθ}/2 − e^{2iθ}/4 passes through −0.75 at θ = π. Test: c = 0 gives the unit circle; connectedness of J_c matches membership of c in M.
Repo: PtolemyDesktop formulary `dmj-ManyJulia` (a grid of Julia sets for many c: "parameter space survey"), mentions in `circle_atlas`, `tier8_sedenion`, `constants`. Status: ESTABLISHED; recipe only.

## 7. Gauge space: the orthogonal Smith charts and the Apollonian gasket  (RECORD + AUTHOR)
Cody (2026-11-19): "the Scale engine: the orthogonal Smith chart, Apollonian gasket hyper object, a type of gauge theory space, the complete shape of scale."
Points: complex numbers read through the Möbius map Γ = (z − 1)/(z + 1), which takes the right half-plane (impedance) onto the unit disc: **the Smith chart**. In the Scale engine's language: Γ_SCALE = tanh(½ ln s) (= (s − 1)/(s + 1), MEASURED equal) ⟂ Γ_ADD = tanh(½ a), with the parity g choosing the sheet (`add_scale_sign.to_smith()`). Axes: **not lines but two orthogonal pencils of circles**: constant-resistance circles (centre r/(r+1), radius 1/(r+1)) and constant-reactance circles (centre 1 + j/x, radius 1/|x|); MEASURED: the r = 1 and x = 1 circles meet at right angles. Both pencils are *parabolic*: every circle in them passes through Γ = 1 (the image of z = ∞), unlike the Apollonian circles of a pair of points, which form a non-intersecting (hyperbolic) pencil with the circles through the two points as its orthogonal family. How the gasket's tangent circles sit relative to these pencils is the Möbius-group statement below, not an identity of the pencils. Reading (author): the chart is the (T, R) section of a Penrose diagram: matched Γ = 0 is the "Now" (σ = ½, J_red = J_blue); |Γ| = 1 is null and spatial infinity; |Γ| > 1 (the active region) is i⁺; z → 1/z (Γ → −Γ) is time reversal J_N. The **Apollonian gasket** is the limit set of a Kleinian group generated by Möbius maps (Sullivan's dictionary: Kleinian groups ↔ rational maps; citable-work index): Smith chart and gasket are two representations of one Möbius-group object ("foldings of the circle"); the gasket as recursive gap-fill is the context-flow / eddy-cascade picture. Gauge reading: the group acting is the Möbius group; the orbit space under SCALE is polar: r (the scale, ordinal) and θ (the invariant).
Motions: Möbius transformations (the hyperbolic isometries of the disc); inversion z → 1/z; scaling s → λs (a translation of Γ_SCALE's argument ln s). Invariant: the cross-ratio; the Poincaré metric on the disc. Views: the chart with both circle pencils drawn; the gasket by iterating the generators; the (ln s, a) strip and its tanh images; parity sheets as two overlaid discs.
Repo: `ValaQuenta/modules/scale`, `add_scale_sign` (`.to_smith()`), `three_ring_scale` (wiki), `circle_atlas`; SFR wiki PW8–PW9 (the Smith chart as design template for the visualiser). Test: orthogonality of the two pencils; Γ(s) = tanh(½ ln s); z → 1/z sends Γ → −Γ.
Not found in `scale/maths.py`: the word "gauge". The gauge reading is the author's; confirm before building anything on it.

## 9. The Higgs field space (the hat)  (ESTABLISHED, implemented in the engine)
Points: values of the doublet H (four real components). Axes: ℝ⁴ ≅ ℂ², with O(4) symmetry; the potential depends on |H|. The vacuum manifold is the brim: a circle in the 2-D slice drawn in the renders, a **3-sphere** in the full doublet. Motions: classical paths (δS = 0) in time, Lorentzian (V) or Euclidean (−V); the hat itself may change in time (`finite_temperature_hat`, `moving_hat_exchange`). Invariants: energy (conserved only for a static hat), the reduced action W along a path. Views: SKILL.md §4 and §10 below.
Engines: `witches_hat` (`action.py`: `ray_bundle`, `brim_bundle`, `tip_report`, `stationary_paths`, `morse_index`, `euclidean_radial_release`), `lagrangian_sm`.

## 10. Rendering rules settled in use (2026-11-19, WitchesHat Lagrangians v2.0)
1. **Show what the maths says, exactly; never draw to resemble a picture the author has.** Find the shape in the computation: integrate the whole family (every source, every direction), select by what each member does (here: passes within ε of the tip), and draw what survives.
2. **One vertical scale from the object's own units** when two kinds of quantity share an axis: energy in units of the depth V₀; action W in units of V₀ τ₀ with τ₀ = R/√(2V₀) (the crossing time at the brim speed); brim at 0, symmetric hilltop at 1; each path's cone starts on the surface at its source and rises by W/(V₀ τ₀). State the convention in the legend: action and energy differ by a time.
3. **Report the number the picture exists to show**: here the height of the tip above the hilltop, its distance from the hilltop in the plane, and the sheets of the multivalued action (SM, E = 0: 0.2189 depth, 0.0015 R, sheets 0.4310 and 0.9025).
4. **Cutaway views**: remove the wedge (isometric) or half (front, side) that faces the camera, and draw the section through the hilltop; otherwise the wall hides the hill and trough.
5. **Size budget**: draw each path as a few short coloured polylines, not one element per sample (10× smaller); target ≤ 5 MB per SVG (v2.0 came in at 2.7–4.6 MB).
6. **Versioned names and a home with the paper**: `WitchesHat_Lagrangians_vX.Y_<name>.svg` under `FourthAgePapers/WitchesHat/papers/figures/lagrangians/vX.Y/`, each version kept; the generating script keeps the version in its name or header.
