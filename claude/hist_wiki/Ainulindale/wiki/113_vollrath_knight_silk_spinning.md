---

- **[VollrathKnight2001]** Vollrath, F. & Knight, D. P. (2001). *Liquid
  crystalline spinning of spider silk.* Nature 410, 541–548.
  doi:10.1038/35069000. — The gland stores spidroin as a concentrated aqueous
  **dope**; the tapered duct applies, in sequence, ion exchange (Na⁺/Cl⁻ out,
  K⁺/PO₄/SO₄ in), acidification, water reabsorption and **elongational shear**;
  the dope passes through a **nematic liquid-crystalline** phase and the
  poly-alanine blocks nucleate β-sheet. Spinning is **pultrusion — drawing,
  not extrusion**.
- **[BlackledgeAgnarsson2010]** Agnarsson, I., Kuntner, M. & Blackledge, T. A.
  (2010). *Bioprospecting finds the toughest biological material: extraordinary
  silk from a giant riverine orb spider.* PLoS ONE 5(9), e11234. — *Caerostris
  darwini* silk toughness (~350–520 MJ/m³); the Veritasium test bench.
- **[Buehler2010]** Keten, S., Xu, Z., Ihle, B. & Buehler, M. J. (2010).
  *Nanoconfinement controls stiffness, strength and mechanical toughness of
  β-sheet crystals in silk.* Nat. Mater. 9, 359–367. — **2–3 nm** β-sheet
  nanocrystals in a semi-amorphous matrix; small crystals out-tough large ones.
