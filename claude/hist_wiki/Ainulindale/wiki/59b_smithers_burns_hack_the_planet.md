## The Fermat Blackboards

Season 7, Episode 6. *Treehouse of Horror VI.* Homer falls into a 3D world.
On the blackboard: `1782¹² + 1841¹² = 1922¹²`

Season 10, Episode 2. *The Wizard of Evergreen Terrace.* Homer writes on a blackboard:
`3987¹² + 4365¹² = 4472¹²`

Both are Fermat near-misses. Both are false. Both pass a 10-digit calculator check.
Both were found by computer program — David X. Cohen searched deliberately for
numbers that would fool a finite-precision instrument.

Fermat's Last Theorem says: no positive integers x, y, z and n > 2 satisfy xⁿ + yⁿ = zⁿ.

to see the boundary.** The forbidden zone is real. The foot is too long to register
the remaining distance. The halving continues below your detection threshold.

This is Zeno's paradox. The bridge has a length. The foot has a length. When the
remaining gap is smaller than the foot, the foot is blind to it. The paradox
resolves not because the halvings stop — but because the measuring instrument
