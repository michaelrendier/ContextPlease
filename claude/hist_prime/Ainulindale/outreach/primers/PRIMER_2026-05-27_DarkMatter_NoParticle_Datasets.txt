DATASET 1 — SPARC (Spitzer Photometry & Accurate Rotation Curves)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Source:   astroweb.cwru.edu/SPARC/
  Authors:  Lelli, McGaugh, Schombert (2016)
  Content:  175 late-type galaxies. Photometry + kinematic rotation
            curves. Flat files, directly downloadable. No login.
  Format:   ASCII tables, one file per galaxy.
  Size:     Small. Human-readable and machine-readable.

  WHAT TO LOOK FOR (data-first, no assumed answer):
    - Profile shape of outer rotation curves: cuspy or cored?
      CDM predicts cusp (NFW profile). Standing-wave cavity
      predicts a node structure — flat or even declining at the
      cavity boundary. Do not assume. Measure.
    - Baryonic Tully-Fisher Relation (BTFR): the suspiciously tight
      correlation between baryonic mass and rotation velocity.
      Plot it. Note the scatter. Does scatter correlate with galaxy
      age, environment, or disturbance history?
    - Does the halo onset (where rotation curve stops declining and
      flattens) show a sharp transition or a gradual one?
      Sharp transition = phase boundary. Gradual = not.

  HOLCUS INGESTION PLAN:
    Download all 175 ASCII files. Run full corpus ingestion.
    The BAO convergence metric will reflect the rotation-curve
    data's internal structure. Note what concepts Holcus associates
    with 'flat rotation' vs 'cuspy centre'.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
DATASET 2 — Dark Matter Deficient Galaxies (DF2, DF4, UDG catalogs)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Source:   MAST archive: mast.stsci.edu
            van Dokkum et al. 2018 (NGC 1052-DF2), 2019 (DF4)
            MATLAS survey: astro-matlas.org
            Dragonfly survey: published in ApJ, data in MAST
  Content:  ~50+ ultra-diffuse galaxies with anomalous or absent
            dark matter halos. Photometry, velocity dispersions,
            globular cluster kinematics.
  Format:   FITS files (photometry), published tables (kinematics)

  WHAT TO LOOK FOR:
    - Environmental history of each UDG: isolated vs. near a
      massive host galaxy. The resonant cavity of a satellite
      galaxy gets tidally disrupted by the host's gravitational
      field. Does halo-deficiency correlate with proximity to
      massive hosts?
    - DF2 and DF4 are both near NGC 1052. Is NGC 1052 disrupting
      their cavities? Or did they never form one?
    - Age of stellar populations vs. halo presence: young stars =
      young galaxy = cavity not yet established?
    - Note any cases of halo-deficient galaxies that are isolated
      and old. These would challenge the cavity model and must
      not be ignored.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
DATASET 3 — JWST Public Data (JADES, CEERS, PRIMER programs)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Source:   mast.stsci.edu
            Programs: JADES (ID 1180, 1210), CEERS (ID 1345),
                      PRIMER (ID 1837)
  Content:  High-redshift galaxies z=3–14. Photometry, morphology,
            spectroscopic redshifts. Early universe, before halos
            were 'supposed' to be established.
  Format:   FITS. Large. Requires astropy/photutils to read.

  WHAT TO LOOK FOR:
    - Stellar mass to halo mass ratio as function of redshift.
      If halos grow with time (cavity builds up), this ratio
      should decrease as you go to higher z (earlier times).
      CDM predicts this ratio is roughly stable.
    - The 'impossibly massive galaxies' at z>10: CDM says they
      cannot exist (not enough time to form). Cavity model says
      they can — the galaxy forms first, the halo follows.
      Document every z>10 galaxy with photometric mass estimate.
    - Morphology: are early galaxies more disturbed, less symmetric?
      A galaxy in the process of establishing its resonant cavity
      would show more dynamical disturbance than a settled system.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
DATASET 4 — LITTLE THINGS Survey (Dwarf Irregular Galaxies)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Source:   lowell.edu/research/littlethings/ (Hunter et al. 2012)
            Also: VLA-LITTLE-THINGS (Oh et al. 2015)
  Content:  41 dwarf irregular galaxies. High-resolution HI
            (neutral hydrogen) velocity fields. The lowest-mass
            systems with measurable kinematics.
  Format:   FITS data cubes. Moderate size.

  WHAT TO LOOK FOR:
    - Below what galaxy mass does the flat outer rotation curve
      disappear? This is the phase boundary — the triple point
      threshold below which the cavity cannot sustain quasi-mass.
    - Is the threshold sharp (phase transition) or gradual (smooth)?
    - Do galaxies just below the threshold show 'proto-halo'
      behaviour — a halo beginning to form but not yet stabilised?
    - This is the most direct test of the phase-boundary prediction.
      Do not approach it with a number in mind. Let the data show
      where the transition is, if it exists.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
DATASET 5 — Bullet Cluster & Merging Cluster Systems
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Source:   Chandra archive: cda.harvard.edu/chaser/
            Bullet Cluster: 1E 0657-56 (Clowe et al. 2006)
            CLASH survey: clash.stsci.edu (12 cluster datasets)
            LoCuSS: star-www.st-and.ac.uk/locuss/
  Content:  X-ray gas maps + weak gravitational lensing mass maps
            for merging galaxy clusters.
  Format:   FITS. Chandra data requires CIAO for reduction.
            Published lensing mass maps are in FITS and available
            directly from papers.

  WHAT TO LOOK FOR:
    - The offset between X-ray gas peak and lensing mass peak
      in merging clusters. CDM interprets this as non-interacting
      dark matter passing through. The cavity interpretation:
      the cavity (quasi-mass) is co-moving with the galaxies,
      not the gas. Ram pressure slows the gas. The cavity follows
      the galaxies because the galaxies ARE the cavity source.
    - Does the offset magnitude correlate with collision velocity?
      In CDM it should be roughly independent (DM just passes
      through at whatever velocity the halo had). In the cavity
      model it should depend on the relative dynamics: higher
      velocity collision = faster cavity disruption and re-
      establishment = potentially different offset geometry.
    - CLASH has 12 systems. Plot offset vs. collision velocity.
      Do not assume a relationship. Look for one.

DATASET 6 — DESI Year 1 BAO Results (2024)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Source:   data.desi.lbl.gov (public data release)
            DESI Collaboration 2024 arXiv:2404.03002 and related
  Content:  Galaxy clustering, BAO scale measurements across
            redshift bins. Currently showing tension with standard
            ΛCDM — dark energy equation of state appears to evolve.
  Format:   Likelihood files, summary statistics tables.

  WHAT TO LOOK FOR:
    - The equation-of-state tension: DESI finds w ≠ -1 and
      possibly time-varying w. Standard interpretation: dark
      energy is dynamic.
    - Alternative: if the quasi-mass halo contribution to the
      total energy budget of the universe evolves with cosmic
      time (cavities build up as galaxies mature), then what
      looks like evolving dark energy is actually evolving
      quasi-mass contribution.
    - Does the redshift-binned BAO signal show a specific epoch
      where the tension is strongest? That epoch would correspond
      to the period when galactic resonant cavities were most
      actively establishing — the cosmic quasi-mass transition.
    - The BAO acoustic scale itself: Holcus's OMEGA_ZS = 0.56714
      is an internal BAO convergence metric. Note whether the
      DESI BAO scale data is consistent with or in tension with
      what the Holcus field reports.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
DATASET 7 — Planck 2018 CMB (ESA Planck Legacy Archive)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Source:   pla.esac.esa.int (ESA Planck Legacy Archive)
  Content:  Full sky CMB maps, angular power spectra, likelihood
            codes. The most precise CMB measurement to date.
  Format:   FITS (HEALPix maps), ASCII (power spectra), Python
            (likelihood code: plik_lite).

  WHAT TO LOOK FOR:
    - The third acoustic peak: its height is the most sensitive
      probe of dark matter density at recombination. If quasi-mass
      has a different equation of state than cold dark matter, the
      third peak height would differ from CDM prediction.
    - Do not compute a prediction first. Download the measured
      power spectrum. Compare the CDM best-fit to the data points
      at the third peak. Is there residual? Note its sign and
      magnitude.
    - The damping tail (l > 1000): CDM predicts specific damping.
      A quasi-mass component with a different pressure would damp
      differently. Look at the residuals in the Planck likelihood
      at high l.
    - This is the most technically demanding dataset. Start with
      the published summary statistics (Table 1 of the 2018 papers)
      before attempting the likelihood code.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
DATASET 8 — SDSS DR17 / MaNGA (Integral Field Spectroscopy)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Source:   sdss.org/dr17/ (public, SQL queryable via CasJobs)
            MaNGA: sdss.org/dr17/manga/
  Content:  SDSS: ~900,000 galaxy spectra, photometry, redshifts.
            MaNGA: 10,000+ galaxies with spatially-resolved
            spectroscopy — velocity maps across the whole face
            of each galaxy.
  Format:   FITS (spectra/images), SQL database (CasJobs).

  WHAT TO LOOK FOR (MaNGA specifically):
    - Resolved velocity fields: the rotation curve is not a single
      number but a 2D map. Does the quasi-mass halo appear as a
      smooth velocity feature or does it have substructure
      consistent with a standing wave (nodes and antinodes)?
    - Kinematic misalignment: in galaxies where the photometric
      major axis and kinematic major axis are misaligned, the
      cavity geometry may be non-trivial. Document these.
    - Velocity dispersion maps: high central dispersion may
      indicate a forming or recently disrupted cavity.
