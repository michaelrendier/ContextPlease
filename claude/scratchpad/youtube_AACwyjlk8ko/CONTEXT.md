## What the video says (its own account)
- Mechanical intelligence = a working mental model of a machine that you run (Kenneth Craik 1943; Mary Hegarty's "mental animation", eyes following a pulley system one connection at a time). Thorndike (1920) named abstract, social and mechanical intelligence. Vanderbilt follow-up (Lubinski): spatial ability predicts patents and publications beyond math and verbal scores.
- A machine as a **relay race**: each part hands the job to the next; a fault is a dropped baton.
- **Three signs** of a fixer: (1) sees parts, not names (functional fixedness; McCaffrey 2012); (2) follows the job instead of chasing the symptom (Rasmussen's *symptomatic* vs *topographic* search: follow the healthy route point by point until the good signal turns bad; Polanyi: "we know more than we can tell"); (3) opens it (Kirsh and Bovair 1984: a picture of how power flows let people derive procedures never taught; Crawford).
- Costs: they cannot see anything as just itself; the repair reflex can land wrong on people ("they wanted company, and they got a repair").
- For non-fixers: memorised fixes fail (Morris and Rouse 1985); spatial skill is trainable (Uttal 2013, 217 studies; the weakest improve most); "I'm just not a mechanical person" works as a lid. **Three principles:** say the chain out loud first; test the middle first (the **half-split method**; 10 good tests narrow 1 000 possibilities to one); start with something you are allowed to break.
- Ends with Pirsig: "the real cycle you're working on is a cycle called yourself."
